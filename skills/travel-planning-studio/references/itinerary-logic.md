# Itinerary logic

Use this reference when turning research into a day-by-day route.

## Build around anchors

Start with booked transport and lodging, then fixed-time reservations, closure windows, and must-do items. These anchors define the feasible geography and time budget.

If travelers arrive separately, design the rendezvous and pre-meeting period before the shared itinerary. If departure date or destination remains open, keep a common core and explicit conditional branches rather than duplicating the whole plan.

## Cluster before sequencing

- Group POIs by walkable area or realistic short transfer.
- Avoid crossing the same city repeatedly.
- Pair meals, cafes, and shopping with nearby anchor activities.
- Treat hotel changes and intercity transfers as activities with their own buffers.
- Use route calculations as evidence, not as a substitute for judgment about fatigue, luggage, terrain, weather, or queues.

## Pace rules

- Plan one anchor per half-day for a moderate trip.
- Add optional items only when they can be skipped without breaking the day.
- Preserve meal, rest, and check-in windows.
- Use larger buffers on holidays, at airports and stations, and for rural transportation.
- Avoid consecutive early starts unless the user explicitly prefers an intensive pace.

## Day structure

For each day include:

- theme and primary area;
- morning, midday, afternoon, and evening sequence;
- estimated transfers and meaningful buffers;
- meal candidate plus nearby backup;
- booked and must-do status;
- bad-weather, queue, closure, or low-energy fallback;
- items that require rechecking or reservation.
- branch conditions and decision deadlines when that day is not yet fixed.

Use exact clock times only when evidence or a booking supports them. Otherwise prefer realistic windows.

## Feasibility checks

Before delivery, verify:

- opening windows overlap the planned visit;
- last admission is not confused with closing time;
- transfers include walking to and from stations or parking;
- luggage and check-in constraints are represented;
- the final activity leaves enough time for the required departure;
- restaurant and attraction backups are genuinely nearby;
- multi-stop routes use the intended travel mode.

## Recovery design

Every fragile day should have a repair path. Examples include swapping indoor and outdoor blocks, dropping the lowest-priority stop, using a closer restaurant, or moving a flexible activity to another day. Make the recovery rule clear enough to use on the trip without re-planning from scratch.
