# Selection frameworks

Use these frameworks to turn a long list of search results into a short list that fits the actual trip. Shortlist neighborhoods and route shapes before individual venues when geography drives the choice.

## Lodging

Choose the area first, then the property. Compare:

- total door-to-door time to the itinerary anchors;
- arrival and departure convenience, including the final late-night or early-morning segment;
- verified room type, occupancy, cancellation policy, taxes, deposits, and breakfast;
- noise, stairs, lifts, parking, accessibility, luggage storage, and check-in constraints;
- recent signals about cleanliness and operational consistency;
- nearby food and transport resilience when plans change.

Do not call a room available, booked, or within budget without current evidence. Keep hotel candidates separate from confirmed reservations.

## Attractions and activities

Evaluate:

- fit with stated interests and whether the experience is locally distinctive;
- true visit duration, queue risk, last admission, closure day, and reservation rules;
- indoor or outdoor exposure, seasonality, terrain, and accessibility;
- geographic fit with meals and adjacent activities;
- value relative to the time cost, not only the ticket price.

Avoid adding famous sights merely to make the plan look complete. Give each optional stop a clear drop rule.

## Food and cafes

Verify the exact branch when possible. Check:

- service hours for the relevant meal and known break periods;
- reservation or queue pattern and group-size limits;
- dietary compatibility, signature dishes, realistic price range, and payment requirements;
- distance from the surrounding activities and a nearby backup;
- whether the recommendation is based on current operation rather than an old article or a similarly named venue.

Do not overload the guide with interchangeable restaurants. Prefer a small, geographically useful set with a reason for each inclusion.

## Shopping, markets, and strolling areas

Distinguish a destination shopping stop from a convenient browse. Consider:

- operating days and the time when the area is actually lively;
- authenticity, price transparency, and tourist-trap risk;
- luggage, shipping, tax-refund, and carrying constraints;
- lighting, weather cover, terrain, and late-evening transport;
- how the stop connects two stronger itinerary anchors.

## Transportation

Use official or operator sources for schedules and rules. Model the full journey:

- origin-to-platform and platform-to-destination time;
- transfers, waiting, check-in, security, parking, and luggage handling;
- operating window and missed-connection recovery;
- child, bicycle, accessibility, reservation, or driving-license constraints;
- rural or late-night last-mile availability;
- coordinate system and provider compatibility for navigation links.

Do not compare modes using only in-vehicle time. Surface the tradeoff among certainty, flexibility, fatigue, cost, and scenery.

For rented bicycles, scooters, motorcycles, cars, or boats, also check legal eligibility, insurance, safety equipment, deposit, pickup and return logistics, road or water conditions, range or fuel, parking, breakdown support, and whether current inventory is actually verified. Read `field-readiness.md` when the mode is central to a travel day.

## Final shortlist rule

For each category, retain only candidates that serve a distinct role: primary choice, lower-cost or lower-effort alternative, weather fallback, or geographic backup. If two options serve the same role, keep the better-supported and better-fitting one.
